Noura's Thoughts ♡

BRANCH Coral Foundation 🌊

Since I've been posting a lot about my time in Curaçao, I thought I'd share a little about the organization that's made this whole experience possible.

I'm spending the summer interning with Branch Coral Restoration, a nonprofit focused on restoring and protecting coral reefs here in Curaçao. They grow corals in underwater nurseries and outplant them onto reefs, while also doing research, education, and community outreach.

As someone who's interested in coral restoration and marine conservation, getting to see this work up close has been incredibly exciting. Everyone has been so welcoming and I've already learned so much.

Curaçao!!! 🐚

I landed in Curaçao five days ago to begin my summer internship, and I'm still a little shocked that I'm actually here.

For months, this trip was something I talked about, planned for, and counted down to. Now my days are filled with coral reefs, dive gear, salty hair, and views of the ocean that still don't feel real.

Today I officially started my Advanced Open Water certification. Diving is one of my favorite things, and getting the chance to improve my skills while living surrounded by reefs feels unreal.

My Fish 🐟

I spend most of my day watching them. I love them so much.

My freshwater tank with mollies and red cherry shrimp has become something I orbit around. I check on them constantly, count them, and watch them swim.

There is something comforting about having this tiny living world right next to me. One day I hope to have a huge saltwater tank with coral and reef fish.

Blank Street Coffee ☕

There’s something oddly fascinating about Blank Street Coffee. On the surface, it's just another minimalist café, but once you look closer it feels more like a tech startup wearing a café costume.

Everything is optimized: the design, the speed, the consistency. Coffee becomes less about tradition and more about efficiency.

It's interesting because it shows how culture can become a system and scale into something completely different.

Maps are art 🗺

Cartography is an art.

Every line, every curve, every choice on a map had to be made by someone. Nothing feels accidental.

I love maps because they sit between science and creativity. They show how people organize space, movement, and connection.

Sylvia Plath's Fig Tree 🌳

I think about Sylvia Plath’s fig tree more often than I probably should.

She describes a fig tree where every fig represents a possible life. The fear is not wanting too much, but being unable to choose anything.

I recognize that feeling: loving too many things and wanting too many versions of yourself.

I don't want my figs to rot. I want to be brave enough to choose something.

Building Boston 🏙

I think one of the reasons I love Boston is because it feels layered. The city is literally built on history.

Back Bay used to be water. The land was created through one of the biggest land-making projects in the country.

The city feels alive because everything is stacked together: old streets, tunnels, memories, and stories.

The Kiss by Gustav Klimt 🎨

I love this painting so much it makes me dizzy sometimes.

The colors, patterns, gold, and softness feel like everything is melting together.

I even got my nails inspired by it because I wanted a tiny piece of that feeling with me.

Mary Shelley 🖤

I've always been fascinated by Mary Shelley, not just because she wrote Frankenstein, but because her life feels almost as gothic as the story itself.

Her life was filled with science, grief, love, and questions about life and death.

She reminds me that stories often come from the strangest and most human parts of ourselves.

Four Eye Butterfly Fish 🐠

I recently learned about four-eye butterflyfish and immediately became obsessed.

Their fake eye helps confuse predators, and their patterns are not just beautiful — they are survival.

The ocean is full of tiny things that look like art.

Women in Greek Mythology 🏛

I've been thinking about women in Greek mythology again.

Pandora, Cassandra, Medusa, and Persephone all have stories where women are blamed, misunderstood, or reduced to symbols.

What fascinates me is looking closer and asking who gets to tell the story, and why certain versions survive.
